1. Preface

1.1 Introduction

Show Sanskrit

सरस्वति नमस्तुभ्यं वरदे कामरूपिणि ।
विद्यारम्भं करिष्यामि सिद्धिर्भवतु मे सदा ॥

O Goddess Saraswati, I bow to you. You are the giver of boons and the fulfiller of wishes. As I begin my studies, please bless me so that I may attain success always.

Before we embark onto the topic of Varna in Sanatana-Dharma we must begin with an investigation into how something may be proven at all within Hinduism as a whole. The scope of this project does not exclude evidence pertaining to data external to Hinduism nor to dogmatically assert internal claims. The preceding sentence will be explained in further detail as the document goes on but was iterated as such to provide a basic pre-print of our method. Let us now investigate some pramanas which we shall reflect upon to conclude how investigation on Varna or Varṇa-vicāra.

Show Sanskrit

यः शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य वर्तते कामकारतः । न स सिद्धिमवाप्नोति न सुखं न परां गतिम् ॥
तस्माच्छास्त्रं प्रमाणं ते कार्याकार्यव्यवस्थितौ । ज्ञात्वा शास्त्रविधानोक्तं कर्म कर्तुमिहार्हसि ॥

The supreme lord said: “He who, discarding scriptural procedure, proceeds as per his own wishes, he does not attain success, neither happiness, nor the highest goal. Therefore, the scripture is your basis in the determination of what is to be done and what is not to be done. Having known the scriptural injunction, it befits you to perform actions in this world.”

—Bhagavad Gītā 16.23–24

Mīmāmsā sūtras, which are the maxims for Scriptural interpretation, state in their very second aphorism:

Show Sanskrit

चोदनालक्षणोऽर्थो धर्मः ॥ १.१.२ ॥

“That which is enjoined by the statement of scripture is dharma.”

—Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.1.2

Yājñavalkya Śikṣā:

Show Sanskrit

युक्तियुक्तं वचो ग्राह्यं न ग्राह्यं गुरुगौरवात् ॥

One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.

Remark 1

Thus, with the support of pramāṇas as well as sound reasoning one may conclude that the consensus amongst ācāryas is no proof of a scriptural stance. The statements, conduct and conclusions of all ācāryas are to be subjected to logic and reasoning and śāstra.

Objection: But ācāryas dedicate many years to studying the Śāstras, and follow a strict paramparā. Hence the consensus of the paramparās is to be taken with confidence.

Reply: This is not so for many issues. If consensus of currently existing paramparās is itself taken as proof, we reject this because many gurus have gone against earlier consensus to establish their sampradāya.

Remark 2

The notion that one needs a paramparā to interpret scripture may fall into a circular chain of reasoning: Śāstra validates a paramparā → Paramparā determines how to interpret śāstra → That interpretation validates the paramparā.

2. Pūrvapakṣa

2.1 Jāti as prerequisite of varṇa

It is said that Varna is directly correlated and predicated upon by Jati. One born as a brahmin-yoni belongs to the Brahmin jati and Brahmin varna and likewise for the other varnas. Birth/jati determines initial varna status and eligibility whereas samskaras and vedic karmas complete and actualise the status required for certain adhikaras.

Remark 3

However being born as a Brahmin will provide certain benefits no matter how indulged in sin you are.

3. Rejection of Jāti

In order to refute the Pūrvapakṣa position we must make use of the maxim:

One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.

Let us begin with Scriptural Pramāṇas against Jāti as a universal prerequisite of Varṇa.

3.1 Mlecchas and Śūdras becoming Brāhmaṇa like Viśvāmitra

Show Sanskrit

विश्वामित्रस्तु राजेन्द्र ब्राह्मणत्वजिगीषया । तपश्चचार विपुलं सन्तापाय दिवौकसाम् ॥ ५६ ॥
ब्राह्मणत्वं न लेभेऽसौ लेभे विघ्नाननेकशः । ततस्तु नियमात्तेषां तिथीनां प्रवरा तिथिः ॥ ५७ ॥
उपोषिता बहुविधा ज्ञात्वा ब्रह्मप्रियां तिथिम् । ततो ददौ ब्रह्मा विश्वामित्राय धीमते । इहैव तेन देहेन ब्राह्मणत्वं सुदुर्लभम् ॥ ५८ ॥
तिथीनां प्रवरा ह्येषा तिथीनामुत्तमा तिथिः । क्षत्रियो वैश्यशूद्रौ वा ब्राह्मणत्वमवाप्नुयुः ॥ ५९ ॥

‘Thereupon Brahmā granted the wise Viśvāmitra the exceedingly difficult attainment of brāhmaṇa status here in this very body.’

The source says that through it a kṣatriya, vaiśya, or śūdra may attain brāhmaṇa status.

—Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 1.16.56–59

Remark 4

Viśvāmitra was not born a Brahmin or Brahmin-jāti. The purāṇa states that Mlecchas and Śūdras can also perform the fast to attain brahmin-hood. We should understand it in the same sense as Viśvāmitra.

3.2 Rejection of Jāti-distinctions in every sense

We shall now examine statements from the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, Brahma Parva which explicitly state that in every sense and every regard there exists no such distinction of Jātis based on birth or family/lineage.

Show Sanskrit

गोवर्गमध्यं च गतो यथाश्वो निर्धार्यते ज्ञैः सुविचक्षणत्वात् । मनुष्यभावादविशिष्यमाणस्तद्वद्द्विजः शूद्रगणान्न भिन्नः ॥ २० ॥
मनुष्यजातेर् न परो विशेषो यः कल्प्यते सर्वनरानुयायी । संस्कारयुक्ता हि क्रियाविशिष्टा द्विजन्मनां शूद्रविवेकहेतुः ॥ २१ ॥

‘There is no further distinction beyond the human species that accompanies every person. Consecratory rites and distinctive actions are what provide the basis for differentiating the twice-born from śūdras.’

—Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 1.40.20–22

Remark 5

The individual soul within a human has no varṇa. There is no perceptual difference between any two humans as all are human by nature. The text states the only way to distinguish is through saṃskāra-yuktā kriyā-viśiṣṭā — distinguished action joined with rites.

Show Sanskrit

तस्मान्न गोश्ववत्कश्चिज्जातिभेदोऽस्ति देहिनाम् । कार्यशक्तिनिमित्तस्तु सङ्केतः कृत्रिमो भवेत् ॥ ३४ ॥

‘Therefore embodied beings possess no birth-class distinction like that between a cow and a horse. The convention is an artificial one based upon aptitude for particular work.’

—Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 1.40.34
