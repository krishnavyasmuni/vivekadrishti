A living textual tradition

From Śruti to the great post-Vedic libraries

The boundaries of “Hindu scripture” are not a single closed canon. Some lists are precise — such as the Muktikā’s 108 Upaniṣads — while Smṛti and Upapurāṇa enumerations differ across texts and schools. This atlas shows both the stable cores and the important variants.

01 · ŚRUTI

Vedic literature

Each Veda is shown as a textual family: surviving Saṃhitā recensions, principal Brāhmaṇas, extant Āraṇyakas, and the Upaniṣads assigned to that Veda in the Muktikā canon.

About the 108: the Muktikā tradition distributes them as 10 Ṛgveda, 16 Sāmaveda, 19 Śukla-Yajurveda, 32 Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda and 31 Atharvaveda. “Principal” badges mark the commonly recognized thirteen major Upaniṣads. Secondary labels such as Yoga, Sannyāsa, Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava and Śākta can vary between modern classifications.

02 · ITIHĀSA

The epic tradition

The two great Itihāsas are narrative, theological and dharma libraries in their own right.

R

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa

Traditionally arranged in seven kāṇḍas: Bāla, Ayodhyā, Araṇya, Kiṣkindhā, Sundara, Yuddha and Uttara.

M

Mahābhārata

Eighteen parvans in the standard architecture. The Bhagavad Gītā occurs within the Bhīṣma-parvan and is one of the epic’s most influential theological texts.

H

Harivaṃśa

A khila or supplement to the Mahābhārata, especially important for Kṛṣṇa and Yādava genealogical narrative; sometimes loosely grouped with Purāṇic literature.

06 · ĀGAMA & ALLIED TRADITIONS

Temple, mantra and tantric corpora

These are enormous, school-specific textual universes and do not reduce to a single all-Hindu list.

Vaiṣṇava Āgama

Pāñcarātra Saṃhitās and Vaikhānasa ritual-text traditions, central to many Vaiṣṇava temple systems.

Śaiva Āgamas

The Śaiva Siddhānta tradition famously recognizes a corpus of twenty-eight principal Āgamas, alongside extensive subsidiary material.

Śākta Tantras

Large families of Devī-centered Tantras and Āgamas, with different canons in Śrīvidyā, Kaula and other traditions.

Upavedas

Traditional auxiliary sciences commonly include Āyurveda, Dhanurveda, Gāndharvaveda and Sthāpatyaveda/related architectural traditions; Vedic associations vary by source.

07 · CANON NOTES

How to read this index

A map of traditions is more accurate than a single rigid tree.

Textual caveats. Vedic schools once possessed many more śākhās and associated Brāhmaṇas than survive today. The seven Āraṇyakas listed here are the extant set commonly recognized by the Vedic Heritage Portal. The Muktikā’s 108 is a particular traditional Upaniṣad canon, not a claim that only 108 Upaniṣads ever existed. Smṛti and Upapurāṇa lists are explicitly variable, so this page presents named enumerations rather than forcing a false universal total.
