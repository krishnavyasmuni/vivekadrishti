Chapter 1

Arjuna Viṣāda Yoga · The Yoga of Arjuna’s Despondency

Chapter 1 introduces the battlefield, the warriors and the crisis that leads Arjuna to seek instruction from Śrī Kṛṣṇa. Each entry keeps the root Sanskrit and English translation visible; the transliterations and the Sanskrit of Śrīdhara Svāmī’s Bhāṣya open from the pill controls below.

The English beneath “Śrīdhara’s Commentary” is a close, fresh translation of the Sanskrit commentary. Where the source records no separate note, that is stated rather than silently supplied.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.1

धृतराष्ट्र उवाच
धर्मक्षेत्रे कुरुक्षेत्रे समवेता युयुत्सवः ।
मामकाः पाण्डवाश्चैव किमकुर्वत सञ्जय ॥ १ ॥

English translation. Dhṛtarāṣṭra said: On the field of dharma, the field of the Kurus, assembled and eager for battle, what did my sons and the sons of Pāṇḍu do, O Sañjaya?

Transliteration

dhṛtarāṣṭra uvāca
dharmakṣetre kurukṣetre samavetā yuyutsavaḥ
māmakāḥ pāṇḍavāścaiva kimakurvata sañjaya

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

इह खलु सकललोकहितावतारः सकलवन्दितचरणः परमकारुणिको भगवान् देवकीनन्दनस्तत्त्वाज्ञानविजृम्भितशोकमोहविभ्रंशितविवेकतया निजधर्मत्यागपरधर्माभिसंधिपरमर्जुनं धर्मज्ञानरहस्योपदेशप्लवेन तस्माच्छोकमोहसागरादुद्दधार। तमेव भगवदुपदिष्टमर्थं कृष्णद्वैपायनः सप्तभिः श्लोकशतैरुपनिबबन्ध। तत्र च प्रायशः श्रीकृष्णमुखनिःसृतानेव श्लोकानलिखत् कांश्चित्तत्संगतये स्वयं व्यरचयत्। यथोक्तं गीतामाहात्म्ये गीता सुगीता कर्तव्या किमन्यैः शास्त्रविस्तरैः। या स्वयं पद्मनाभस्य मुखपद्माद्विनिःसृता।। इति। तत्र तावद्धर्मक्षेत्र इत्यादिना विषीदन्निदमब्रवीदित्यन्तेन ग्रन्थेन श्रीकृष्णार्जुनसंवादप्रस्तावाय कथा निरूप्यते धर्मक्षेत्र इति। भो संजय धर्मभूमौ कुरुक्षेत्रे मत्पुत्राः पाण्डुपुत्राश्च युयुत्सवो योद्धुमिच्छन्तः समवेता मिलिताः सन्तः किं कृतवन्तः।

Śrīdhara transliteration

iha khalu sakalalokahitāvatāraḥ sakalavanditacaraṇaḥ paramakāruṇiko bhagavān devakīnandanastattvājñānavijṛmbhitaśokamohavibhraṃśitavivekatayā nijadharmatyāgaparadharmābhisaṃdhiparamarjunaṃ dharmajñānarahasyopadeśaplavena tasmācchokamohasāgarāduddadhāra| tameva bhagavadupadiṣṭamarthaṃ kṛṣṇadvaipāyanaḥ saptabhiḥ ślokaśatairupanibabandha| tatra ca prāyaśaḥ śrīkṛṣṇamukhaniḥsṛtāneva ślokānalikhat kāṃścittatsaṃgataye svayaṃ vyaracayat| yathoktaṃ gītāmāhātmye gītā sugītā kartavyā kimanyaiḥ śāstravistaraiḥ| yā svayaṃ padmanābhasya mukhapadmādviniḥsṛtā|| iti| tatra tāvaddharmakṣetra ityādinā viṣīdannidamabravīdityantena granthena śrīkṛṣṇārjunasaṃvādaprastāvāya kathā nirūpyate dharmakṣetra iti| bho saṃjaya dharmabhūmau kurukṣetre matputrāḥ pāṇḍuputrāśca yuyutsavo yoddhumicchantaḥ samavetā militāḥ santaḥ kiṃ kṛtavantaḥ|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Here, indeed, the supremely compassionate Lord, Devakī’s son, whose feet are worshipped by all and who descended for the good of the entire world, lifted Arjuna—whose discernment had been destroyed by sorrow and delusion arising from ignorance of truth, and who was intent on abandoning his own dharma and taking up another’s—from the ocean of sorrow and delusion by the vessel of the secret teaching of dharma and knowledge. Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana [Vyāsa] composed the very meaning taught by the Lord in seven hundred verses; for the most part he wrote verses that came from Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s mouth, and composed some himself for their connection. As said in the Gītā-māhātmya: “The Gītā should be made well-sung; what need is there for other extensive scriptures, since it itself came from the lotus-mouth of Padmanābha?” Thus, through the narrative beginning with “the field of dharma” and ending with “he spoke this,” the story is described to introduce the dialogue between Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna. “In the field of dharma...” O Sañjaya, in Kurukṣetra, the field of dharma, my sons and the sons of Pāṇḍu, desirous of fighting, having assembled—what did they do?

Bhagavad Gītā 1.2

सञ्जय उवाच
दृष्ट्वा तु पाण्डवानीकं व्यूढं दुर्योधनस्तदा ।
आचार्यमुपसंगम्य राजा वचनमब्रवीत् ॥ २ ॥

English translation. Sañjaya said: Seeing the Pāṇḍava army arrayed for battle, King Duryodhana then approached his teacher and spoke these words.

Transliteration

sañjaya uvāca
dṛṣṭvā tu pāṇḍavānīkaṃ vyūḍhaṃ duryodhanastadā
ācāryamupasaṃgamya rājā vacanamabravīt

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

दृष्ट्वेति। पाण्डवानामनीकं सैन्यं व्यूढं व्यूहरचनया व्यवस्थितं दृष्ट्वा द्रोणाचार्यसमीपं गत्वा राजा दुर्योधनो वक्ष्यमाणं वाक्यमुवाच।

Śrīdhara transliteration

dṛṣṭveti| pāṇḍavānāmanīkaṃ sainyaṃ vyūḍhaṃ vyūharacanayā vyavasthitaṃ dṛṣṭvā droṇācāryasamīpaṃ gatvā rājā duryodhano vakṣyamāṇaṃ vākyamuvāca|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

On “seeing”: Having seen the Pāṇḍavas’ army, arranged and stationed through its battle formation, King Duryodhana went to Ācārya Droṇa and spoke the words that follow.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.3

पश्यैतां पाण्डुपुत्राणामाचार्य महतीं चमूम् ।
व्यूढां द्रुपदपुत्रेण तव शिष्येण धीमता ॥ ३ ॥

English translation. Behold, O teacher, this great army of the sons of Pāṇḍu, arrayed by the intelligent son of Drupada, your disciple.

Transliteration

paśyaitāṃ pāṇḍuputrāṇāmācārya mahatīṃ camūm
vyūḍhāṃ drupadaputreṇa tava śiṣyeṇa dhīmatā

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तदेव वाक्यमाह पश्यैतामित्यादिनवभिः श्लोकैः। भो आचार्य पाण्डवानां विततां चमूं सेनां पश्य। द्रुपदपुत्रेण धृष्टद्युम्नेन व्यूढां व्यूहरचनया अधिष्ठिताम्।

Śrīdhara transliteration

tadeva vākyamāha paśyaitāmityādinavabhiḥ ślokaiḥ| bho ācārya pāṇḍavānāṃ vitatāṃ camūṃ senāṃ paśya| drupadaputreṇa dhṛṣṭadyumnena vyūḍhāṃ vyūharacanayā adhiṣṭhitām|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

He states those very words in the nine verses beginning with “Behold.” O teacher, behold this extensive army of the Pāṇḍavas, arranged by Dhṛṣṭadyumna, the intelligent son of Drupada and your disciple.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.4

अत्र शूरा महेष्वासा भीमार्जुनसमा युधि ।
युयुधानो विराटश्च द्रुपदश्च महारथः ॥ ४ ॥

English translation. Here are heroic bowmen, equal in battle to Bhīma and Arjuna: Yuyudhāna, Virāṭa, and the great chariot-warrior Drupada.

Transliteration

atra śūrā maheṣvāsā bhīmārjunasamā yudhi
yuyudhāno virāṭaśca drupadaśca mahārathaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अत्र शूरा इति। अत्रास्यां चम्वां इषवो बाणा अस्यन्ते क्षिप्यन्ते एभिरितीष्वासाः धनूंषि। महान्त इष्वासा येषां ते तथा। भीमार्जुनौ तावदत्रातिप्रसिद्धौ योद्धारौ ताभ्यां समाः शूराः शौर्येण क्षात्रधर्मेणोपेताः सन्ति। तानेव नामाभिर्निर्दिशति। युयुधानः सात्यकिः।

Śrīdhara transliteration

atra śūrā iti| atrāsyāṃ camvāṃ iṣavo bāṇā asyante kṣipyante ebhiritīṣvāsāḥ dhanūṃṣi| mahānta iṣvāsā yeṣāṃ te tathā| bhīmārjunau tāvadatrātiprasiddhau yoddhārau tābhyāṃ samāḥ śūrāḥ śauryeṇa kṣātradharmeṇopetāḥ santi| tāneva nāmābhirnirdiśati| yuyudhānaḥ sātyakiḥ|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Here, in this army, are heroes with great bows—“bows” are so called because arrows are shot from them. Those whose bows are great are equal to Bhīma and Arjuna, famous warriors here, possessing valor and the warrior’s dharma. He names them: Yuyudhāna is Sātyaki.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.5

धृष्टकेतुश्चेकितानः काशिराजश्च वीर्यवान् ।
पुरुजित्कुन्तिभोजश्च शैब्यश्च नरपुंगवः ॥ ५ ॥

English translation. Dhṛṣṭaketu, Cekitāna, the valiant king of Kāśī, Purujit, Kuntibhoja, and Śaibya, the best of men;

Transliteration

dhṛṣṭaketuścekitānaḥ kāśirājaśca vīryavān
purujitkuntibhojaśca śaibyaśca narapuṃgavaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

किंच धृष्टकेतुरिति। चेकितानो नाम एको राजा। नरपुङ्गवो नरश्रेष्ठः शैब्यः।

Śrīdhara transliteration

kiṃca dhṛṣṭaketuriti| cekitāno nāma eko rājā| narapuṅgavo naraśreṣṭhaḥ śaibyaḥ|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Moreover, Dhṛṣṭaketu and Cekitāna, a certain king named Cekitāna, the valiant king of Kāśī, Purujit, Kuntibhoja, and Śaibya, the best of men.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.6

युधामन्युश्च विक्रान्त उत्तमौजाश्च वीर्यवान् ।
सौभद्रो द्रौपदेयाश्च सर्व एव महारथाः ॥ ६ ॥

English translation. Yudhāmanyu, the valiant, and the mighty Uttamaujas; Subhadrā’s son and the sons of Draupadī—all great chariot-warriors.

Transliteration

yudhāmanyuśca vikrānta uttamaujāśca vīryavān
saubhadro draupadeyāśca sarva eva mahārathāḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

युधामन्युश्चेति। विक्रान्तो युधामन्युर्नामैकः। सौभद्रोऽभिमन्युः। द्रौपदेयाः द्रोपद्यां पञ्चभ्यो युधिष्ठिरादिभ्यो जाताः प्रतिविन्ध्यादयः पञ्च। महारथादीनां लक्षणम् एको दशसहस्त्राणि योधयेद्यस्तु धन्विनाम्। शस्त्रशास्त्रप्रवीणश्च महारथ इति स्मृतः।।अमितान्यो धयेद्यस्तु संप्रोक्तोऽतिरथस्तु सः। रथी त्वेकेन यो युद्ध्येत्तन्न्यूनोऽर्धरथी मतः।। इति।

Śrīdhara transliteration

yudhāmanyuśceti| vikrānto yudhāmanyurnāmaikaḥ| saubhadro’bhimanyuḥ| draupadeyāḥ dropadyāṃ pañcabhyo yudhiṣṭhirādibhyo jātāḥ prativindhyādayaḥ pañca| mahārathādīnāṃ lakṣaṇam eko daśasahastrāṇi yodhayedyastu dhanvinām| śastraśāstrapravīṇaśca mahāratha iti smṛtaḥ||amitānyo dhayedyastu saṃprokto’tirathastu saḥ| rathī tvekena yo yuddhyettannyūno’rdharathī mataḥ|| iti|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Yudhāmanyu is one who is valiant; Uttamaujas is powerful; Saubhadra is Abhimanyu; the Draupadeyas are the five sons of Draupadī, Prativindhya and the others. The characteristic of a mahāratha is stated: one who can fight ten thousand bowmen is remembered as a mahāratha; one who can fight an unlimited number is called an atiratha; a ratha fights one, and one inferior to that is considered an ardharatha.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.7

अस्माकं तु विशिष्टा ये तान्निबोध द्विजोत्तम ।
नायका मम सैन्यस्य संज्ञार्थं तान्ब्रवीमि ते ॥ ७ ॥

English translation. Know also, O best of the twice-born, those distinguished among us, the leaders of my army; I name them to you for your understanding.

Transliteration

asmākaṃ tu viśiṣṭā ye tānnibodha dvijottama
nāyakā mama sainyasya saṃjñārthaṃ tānbravīmi te

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अस्माकमिति। निबोध बुध्यस्व। नायका नेतारः। संज्ञार्थं सम्यग्ज्ञानार्थमित्यर्थः।

Śrīdhara transliteration

asmākamiti| nibodha budhyasva| nāyakā netāraḥ| saṃjñārthaṃ samyagjñānārthamityarthaḥ|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

On “ours”: Understand clearly. The leaders are commanders; “for the sake of designation” means for complete knowledge.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.8

भवान्भीष्मश्च कर्णश्च कृपश्च समितिञ्जयः ।
अश्वत्थामा विकर्णश्च सौमदत्तिस्तथैव च ॥ ८ ॥

English translation. Yourself, Bhīṣma, Karṇa, Kṛpa victorious in battle, Aśvatthāmā, Vikarṇa, and Bhūriśravā, the son of Somadatta;

Transliteration

bhavānbhīṣmaśca karṇaśca kṛpaśca samitiñjayaḥ
aśvatthāmā vikarṇaśca saumadattistathaiva ca

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तानेवाह भवानिति द्वाभ्याम्। भवान्द्रोणः। समितिं संग्रामं जयतीति तथा। सौमदत्तिः सोमदत्तस्य पुत्रो भूरिश्रवाः।

Śrīdhara transliteration

tānevāha bhavāniti dvābhyām| bhavāndroṇaḥ| samitiṃ saṃgrāmaṃ jayatīti tathā| saumadattiḥ somadattasya putro bhūriśravāḥ|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

He names them in two verses beginning “Yourself”: You are Droṇa; “victorious in battle” means one who conquers an army. Saumadatti is Bhūriśravā, the son of Somadatta.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.9

अन्ये च बहवः शूरा मदर्थे त्यक्तजीविताः ।
नानाशस्त्रप्रहरणाः सर्वे युद्धविशारदाः ॥ ९ ॥

English translation. and many other heroes who have given up their lives for my sake, armed with many kinds of weapons and all skilled in war.

Transliteration

anye ca bahavaḥ śūrā madarthe tyaktajīvitāḥ
nānāśastrapraharaṇāḥ sarve yuddhaviśāradāḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अन्ये चेति। मदर्थे मत्प्रयोजनार्थं जीवितं त्यक्तुमध्यवसिता इत्यर्थः। नाना अनेकानि शस्त्राणि प्रहरणसाधनानि येषां ते। युद्धे विशारदाः। निपुणा इत्यर्थः।

Śrīdhara transliteration

anye ceti| madarthe matprayojanārthaṃ jīvitaṃ tyaktumadhyavasitā ityarthaḥ| nānā anekāni śastrāṇi praharaṇasādhanāni yeṣāṃ te| yuddhe viśāradāḥ| nipuṇā ityarthaḥ|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Others too: They have resolved to give up their lives for my sake, that is, for my purpose. They are equipped with various weapons and skilled in war, meaning expert.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.10

अपर्याप्तं तदस्माकं बलं भीष्माभिरक्षितम् ।
पर्याप्तं त्विदमेतेषां बलं भीमाभिरक्षितम् ॥ १० ॥

English translation. Our force, protected by Bhīṣma, is not enough to overcome them; while their force, protected by Bhīma, is enough.

Transliteration

aparyāptaṃ tadasmākaṃ balaṃ bhīṣmābhirakṣitam
paryāptaṃ tvidameteṣāṃ balaṃ bhīmābhirakṣitam

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ततः किमित्यत आह अपर्याप्तमिति। तत् तथाभूतैर्वीरैयुक्तमपि भीष्मेणाभिरक्षितमप्यस्माकं बलं सैन्यमपर्याप्तं तैः सह योद्धुमसमर्थं भाति। इदं तु एतेषां पाण्डवानां बलं भीमेनाभिरक्षितं सत् पर्याप्तं समर्थं भाति। भीष्मस्योभयपक्षपातित्वात्। अस्मद्बलं पाण्डवसैन्यं प्रत्यसमर्थम्। भीमस्यैकपक्षपातित्वात्।

Śrīdhara transliteration

tataḥ kimityata āha aparyāptamiti| tat tathābhūtairvīraiyuktamapi bhīṣmeṇābhirakṣitamapyasmākaṃ balaṃ sainyamaparyāptaṃ taiḥ saha yoddhumasamarthaṃ bhāti| idaṃ tu eteṣāṃ pāṇḍavānāṃ balaṃ bhīmenābhirakṣitaṃ sat paryāptaṃ samarthaṃ bhāti| bhīṣmasyobhayapakṣapātitvāt| asmadbalaṃ pāṇḍavasainyaṃ pratyasamartham| bhīmasyaikapakṣapātitvāt|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Then what follows? He says: Although our army is endowed with such heroes and protected by Bhīṣma, it appears inadequate, unable to fight them. But the army of the Pāṇḍavas, protected by Bhīma, appears sufficient, capable—because Bhīṣma favors both sides, whereas Bhīma favors only one side. Thus our army is incapable against the Pāṇḍava army.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.11

अयनेषु च सर्वेषु यथाभागमवस्थिताः ।
भीष्ममेवाभिरक्षन्तु भवन्तः सर्व एव हि ॥ ११ ॥

Transliteration

ayaneṣu ca sarveṣu yathābhāgamavasthitāḥ

Śrīdhara transliteration

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Therefore he says how you should act: stationed at the entrances to the formations, each in his assigned division and without abandoning his own battlefield, let all of you protect Bhīṣma from every side, so that while he fights others he is not killed from behind. आचार्यान्मातुलान्भ्रातॄन्पुत्रान्पौत्रान्सखींस्तथा ॥ २६ ॥

English translation. There Arjuna saw fathers, grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends;

Transliteration

tatrāpaśyatsthitānpārthaḥ pitṝnatha pitāmahān
ācāryānmātulānbhrātṝnputrānpautrānsakhīṃstathā

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ततः किं प्रवृत्तमित्यत आह। पितृ़न्। पितृव्यादीनित्यर्थः। पुत्रान्पौत्रानिति। दुर्योधनादीनां ये पुत्राः पौत्राश्च तानित्यर्थः। सखीन्मित्राणि। सुहृदः कृतोपकारांश्चापश्यत्।

Śrīdhara transliteration

tataḥ kiṃ pravṛttamityata āha| pitṛ़n| pitṛvyādīnityarthaḥ| putrānpautrāniti| duryodhanādīnāṃ ye putrāḥ pautrāśca tānityarthaḥ| sakhīnmitrāṇi| suhṛdaḥ kṛtopakārāṃścāpaśyat|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Then, asked what happened, he says: “Fathers” means paternal uncles and the like. “Sons and grandsons” means the sons and grandsons of Duryodhana and the others. “Friends” means companions; he also saw benefactors who had done him favors.

Bhagavad Gītā 1.36

निहत्य धार्तराष्ट्रान्नः का प्रीतिः स्याज्जनार्दन ।
पापमेवाश्रयेदस्मान्हत्वैतानाततायिनः ॥ ३६ ॥

English translation. What joy would be ours after killing the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, O Janārdana? Sin alone would take hold of us after killing these aggressors.

Transliteration

nihatya dhārtarāṣṭrānnaḥ kā prītiḥ syājjanārdana
pāpamevāśrayedasmānhatvaitānātatāyinaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ननु च अग्निदो गरदश्चैव शस्त्रपाणिर्धनापहः। क्षेत्रदारापहर्ता च षडेते ह्याततायिनः।। इति स्मरणात्। अग्निद इत्यादिभिः षड्भिरपि हेतुभिरेते तावदाततायिनः। आततायिनां च वधो युक्त एव।आततायिनमायान्तं हन्यादेवाविचारयन्। नाततायिवधे दोषो हन्तुर्भवति कश्चन।। इति वचनात्तत्राह सार्धेन पापमिति। अततायिनमायान्तम् इत्यादिकमर्थशास्त्रं धर्मशास्त्राद्दुर्बलम्। यथोक्तं याज्ञवल्क्येन स्मृत्योर्विरोधे न्यायस्तु बलवान्व्यवहारतः। अर्थशास्त्रात्तु बलवद्धर्मशास्त्रमिति स्थितिः।। तस्मादाततायिनामप्येतेषामार्यादीनां वधेऽस्माकं पापमेव भवेदन्याय्यत्वादधर्मत्वाच्चैतद्वधस्य न चेह सुखं स्यादित्याह स्वजनं हीति।

Śrīdhara transliteration

nanu ca agnido garadaścaiva śastrapāṇirdhanāpahaḥ| kṣetradārāpahartā ca ṣaḍete hyātatāyinaḥ|| iti smaraṇāt| agnida ityādibhiḥ ṣaḍbhirapi hetubhirete tāvadātatāyinaḥ| ātatāyināṃ ca vadho yukta eva|ātatāyinamāyāntaṃ hanyādevāvicārayan| nātatāyivadhe doṣo hanturbhavati kaścana|| iti vacanāttatrāha sārdhena pāpamiti| atatāyinamāyāntam ityādikamarthaśāstraṃ dharmaśāstrāddurbalam| yathoktaṃ yājñavalkyena smṛtyorvirodhe nyāyastu balavānvyavahārataḥ| arthaśāstrāttu balavaddharmaśāstramiti sthitiḥ|| tasmādātatāyināmapyeteṣāmāryādīnāṃ vadhe’smākaṃ pāpameva bhavedanyāyyatvādadharmatvāccaitadvadhasya na ceha sukhaṃ syādityāha svajanaṃ hīti|

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

Someone may object: according to the remembered verse “the arsonist, poisoner, armed attacker, robber of wealth, usurper of land and wife—these six are aggressors,” these are aggressors for all six reasons. Killing aggressors is proper, as the statement says: “An approaching aggressor should be killed without consideration; in killing an aggressor the killer incurs no fault.” He replies with the half-verse beginning “sin alone”: the rule of the Arthaśāstra, beginning “an approaching aggressor...,” is weaker than the Dharmaśāstra. As Yājñavalkya says, “When smṛtis conflict, reasoning prevails in practical matters; the Dharmaśāstra is stronger than the Arthaśāstra.” Therefore, even in killing these Āryas and others who are aggressors, sin alone would be ours, since the killing would be unjust and contrary to dharma. Nor would there be happiness here; hence he says, “How could we...?”

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

An objection: Since the fault of killing relatives is equally present for you and for them, just as they proceed to battle accepting that fault, let you proceed likewise—why this despondency? He replies with the next two verses beginning “Even if”: although Duryodhana and the others do not see the fault, their minds are overcome and their discernment ruined by greed for the kingdom, how could we, who clearly see the fault, not know to turn away from this sin? Intelligence must be directed toward withdrawal.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary

With the words “I do not wish to kill them” (1.35), “If I were unresisting...” (1.46), and so on, he states Arjuna’s intended meaning through “in every way.” “In every way” means in many ways: even though they are aggressors; even though they are now ready to kill; even though withdrawal from battle is said to be the cause of adharma and infamy; even though battle is a means to sovereignty over the three worlds; and, what is more, even though you, the Lord of all lords, who give me the most beneficial instruction, have told me [to fight]—this is the purport. The certainty that the destruction of relatives is inevitable is the cause of grief; or the word “grief” here is concerned only with despondency. That grief is shown in reverse order as the cause of abandoning bow and arrows. “His mind overcome” means that his resolve to fight was thoroughly shaken. The verbal root “vijī” means fear and movement. Since his resolve to fight was shaken, he threw down his bow with arrows, like one intent on fasting unto death, withdrew from the warrior’s seat to the lap of the chariot, and sat down.
